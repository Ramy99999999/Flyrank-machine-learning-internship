Predicting Content Risk: A Decision-Support Model for Prioritizing Pages That May Go Dark

A ranked-review-queue experiment: can February 2026 signals help a content team decide which pages to look at first for March 2026? Reported honestly, including where a simple baseline did better.

Research → Data → Method → Evidence → Recommendation

1Abstract

This study asks whether content items can be ranked by their risk of going dark in the following month, so that a content team can decide which pages to review first. Using the FlyRank pseudonymized warehouse release, we use February 2026 search and engagement signals to predict a March 2026 outcome for 29,362 content items (14 columns), of which 1,159 (about 3.95%) went dark. We train a Logistic Regression model with median imputation and standardization and compare it with an inverse-log-impressions baseline, 1 / log1p(gsc_impressions_feb), using a stratified, deterministically ordered split (23,489 train and 5,873 test rows, random_state=42) and Precision@K on held-out data. On the held-out test set the two methods matched at Precision@10 (0.10 each), while the baseline outperformed Logistic Regression at Precision@20 (0.20 vs 0.05) and Precision@50 (0.16 vs 0.10). In this experiment, therefore, the simple baseline is the stronger ranking benchmark, and the scores are best used directionally as decision-support for a human-reviewed queue rather than as evidence that the model adds value.

2Introduction / Problem Statement

A content team cannot review every page equally. The practical goal is a ranked queue that helps prioritize which content items deserve review first, based on their estimated risk of going dark in the following month.

Research question: Can we rank content items by their risk of going dark in the following month, so that a content team can prioritize which pages to review first?

What this is

A prioritization tool and decision-support system. A high score means “review this item earlier.”

What this is not

Not an automated decision to delete, rewrite, or refresh content. Not a claim that a page will definitely fail, that refreshing improves traffic, that any causal effect exists, or that the model explains Google’s ranking factors.

3Data

The data come from the FlyRank Internship — Pseudonymized Warehouse Release (v20260703). The main source is fact_content_daily_performance, with dimensions dim_content and dim_clients. The grain is report_date × client_hash_id × content_hash_id. February 2026 is the feature window and March 2026 is the outcome window. The latest _sample partition is sealed and excluded from model development and evaluation.

Modeling dataset and outcome

The final modeling dataset has 29,362 rows × 14 columns. The outcome went_dark equals 1 when March has measured GSC activity (gsc_measured_days_mar > 0) and total March GSC clicks are zero; otherwise it is 0 under this modeling definition.

Universe filters (eligibility rules)

  • February GSC impressions ≥ 100
  • February GSC clicks ≥ 3
  • Content is published and was created on or before 2026-02-28
  • Deleted content excluded (is_deleted = FALSE)

4Methodology

Features

Five February 2026 features: gsc_impressions_feb, gsc_clicks_feb, avg_position_feb, sessions_organic_feb, scroll_events_feb. Missingness indicators are added for sessions_organic_feb and scroll_events_feb. client_hash_id and content_hash_id are used only for grouping, ordering, and splitting; they are not model features.

Baseline

1 / np.log1p(gsc_impressions_feb)

A simple heuristic that ranks lower-impression content higher. It is intentionally simple and serves as a benchmark for whether the machine-learning model adds value.

Model

Logistic Regression in a pipeline: (1) median imputation, (2) StandardScaler, (3) LogisticRegression with max_iter=1000, random_state=42, class_weight="balanced".

Train/test split

The split is stratified with random_state=42. Before splitting, rows are sorted deterministically so the underlying Parquet/DuckDB row order cannot influence the result:

modeling_df = modeling_df.sort_values(

Leakage prevention

  • February features predict a March outcome; the target comes from the later window.
  • No March outcome information is used as a feature.
  • IDs are not features.
  • The latest _sample partition is excluded from development and evaluation.
  • Evaluation uses held-out data.

The workflow includes explicit leakage checks and time-separated feature and outcome windows. This page does not claim that every possible form of leakage has been ruled out.

Evaluation metric

Precision@K asks: among the top K items ranked as highest risk, how many actually went dark?

Precision@K = (true positives in the top K) / K

Interpretation

The Logistic Regression model did not outperform the simple inverse-log-impressions baseline on the held-out ranking evaluation. At the top 10 the methods matched. At the top 20 and top 50 the baseline scored higher. For this experiment, the simple baseline provides a stronger ranking benchmark than Logistic Regression at K=20 and K=50, and this experiment does not demonstrate that Logistic Regression improves ranking quality over the baseline. This is a valid research result and is reported as such.

  • Held-out Precision@K results
  • The baseline vs Logistic Regression difference
  • The ~3.95% outcome rate
  • Missingness in GA4-derived fields
  • The data-quality check results
Directional

Directional use

Scores can be used directionally to prioritize review.

Decision-support

Decision-support

The ranking can support a content team’s review queue, with humans making the decisions.

Not established

Not established

  • Causality
  • That refreshing content increases traffic
  • That a page will definitely go dark
  • That the model explains Google’s ranking algorithm
  • That the model generalizes to every future period
  • That Logistic Regression is better than the baseline

Additional caveats

  • The positive class is rare (~3.95%), so Precision@K at small K rests on few positive cases.
  • GA4-derived fields have substantial missingness (20,335 rows each).
  • The experiment covers one specific feature/outcome window and one held-out evaluation.
  • The model is intentionally simple.
  • More future validation is needed before operational deployment.
  • Because the baseline outperformed the ML model, additional feature and model work may be necessary.

7Ranked Recommendations

A review playbook, in priority order. It does not assert causal reasons for any outcome.

  1. Review the highest-ranked items first. Use the ranking as a triage queue: top-ranked content gets earlier human review. The score is a prioritization signal, not an automatic action.
  2. Inspect low-impression / low-visibility content. Because the baseline ranked lower-impression content more highly and performed strongly in this experiment, impression volume is a useful signal for prioritization in this particular evaluation. When reviewing, consider relevance, freshness, content quality, search-intent alignment, and whether the page is still strategically useful. These factors are review prompts, not established causes.
  3. Investigate missing engagement data. sessions_organic_feb and scroll_events_feb are each missing for 20,335 rows. Missingness should not be read as zero engagement; where possible, check data coverage before using these signals operationally.
  4. Keep a human review step. Do not automatically rewrite, delete, merge, or prune content based solely on the score. The score determines review priority only.
  5. Validate the next model before deployment. Since Logistic Regression did not beat the baseline at K=20 or K=50, future work can test whether feature engineering, model selection, calibration, or additional time windows add genuine value. A more complex model is not guaranteed to perform better.
